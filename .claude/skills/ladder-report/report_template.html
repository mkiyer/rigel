Rigel · gDNA ladder benchmark

End-to-end accuracy on the ladder

Sixteen simulated libraries of 10,000,000 fragments each, spanning 0–98 % genomic-DNA contamination × two strand specificities × capture on and off. Every figure is scored against the simulator’s own realised per-fragment truth — each fragment counted exactly once, never a pre-capture molar abundance — so a difference is a fragment the tool put somewhere the simulator did not.

How to read this

  • Three pools, three different questions. gDNA is contamination the deconvolution must remove. Nascent RNA is the synthetic entity the index manufactures for each multi-exon gene. Annotated RNA is the transcript table a user reads. The split is on is_synthetic, never is_nrna — a single-exon annotated transcript is annotated RNA, not nascent.
  • The gDNA estimate includes intergenic fragments. They reach no locus and never enter the EM, but they are gDNA and the truth counts them. Off capture they are more than half of it.
  • Percent error is undefined where the truth is zero and is shown n/a. At the 0 % rung there is no gDNA at all; the raw count beside it is the whole of the answer.
  • Read per stratum, never pooled. Three strata are in scope. Unstranded × capture ON is deferred — reported everywhere, never a development target — and it carries most of the error, so a pooled total would mostly be its total.
  • Nothing below the seed floor is attributable. It is printed beside every transcript row. ⚠ Re-running the identical command moves these figures by the same order — four runs of g50 ss.99 OFF spanned 145 fragments against a floor of ±144 — and it is not the seed: it persists with the seed pinned and shrinks to 12.5 fragments only at em.n_threads=1 under OMP_NUM_THREADS=1. Treat every figure here as carrying that much noise, and never act on a smaller difference.
  • expressed and detected are the scored sets. The truth table is larger than either: it carries a row per synthetic nascent entity too (6,919 of 15,669), and those are zero on both sides, so they enter no figure here. A raw row count would read as thousands of transcripts scored perfectly. Likewise only 2,466 of the 9,385 gene-level rows are real genes.
  • There is no false-negative column, because it cannot fire. A false negative needs an estimate of exactly zero, and a fractional posterior never is — it reads 0 on all 16 conditions. Under-assigned is the live quantity and is shown instead.
  • MARD is symmetric — |est−true| / (|est|+|true|) over the expressed set, bounded in [0,1]. 0.5 means a 3× error, not 50 %. Both it and the median relative error are unweighted by mass: a 1-fragment transcript counts as much as a 25,000-fragment one.
  • One asymmetry in the pool rows. The estimate splits on is_synthetic (the entity); the truth splits on the simulator's template kind. A few annotated single-exon transcripts serve as their own nascent entity, so ~300 of ~1.9 M fragments count as nascent truth while the tool's counts for them land in annotated. Recorded, not removable.

02

Transcript level

Inside the annotated pool alone — gDNA and the synthetic nascent entities are excluded, so what is left is how well Rigel splits the RNA it kept. Σ|Δ| sums |estimate − truth| over every transcript in the truth table, silent ones included; the percentage is that sum against the true annotated mass.

03

Gene level

The same scorer over genes, and the difference from the transcript row is the point: summing a gene’s isoforms collapses exactly the error that comes from not knowing which isoform a fragment came from. What survives is error in deciding whether the fragment was RNA from this gene at all — the question Rigel exists to answer.
